Minefield Protocol

A pass-and-play board game about planning under uncertainty.

Minefield Protocol is an offline board game built with plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. Players secretly program actions, then watch those plans resolve across a hidden 10 x 10 minefield.

Players3-8 seats, with optional local bots
FormatPass-and-play, private planning, public resolution
GoalSurvive, clear the bombs, and out-plan the table

Getting started

  1. Open index.html in Chrome, Edge, or Firefox, then choose Game Config. No installation or local server is required.
  2. Choose the number of seats, starting health, terrain, bomb density, and grid visibility.
  3. Select a Mutator, then choose Start Game.
  4. Roll action points in playing order. After all rolls are public, program actions privately.

For a solo game, set Human players to 1 human - play solo. Bots fill the remaining seats and use the same visible information as human players.

Players and bots

The game supports 3-8 seats. All-human games pass the computer between players; mixed games combine human seats with local bots.

  • New Player: wanders, takes risks, and sometimes misses tactical opportunities.
  • Moderate: pursues opponents, routes around visible mines, and takes useful pushes.
  • Expert: prepares unknown ground with Disarm and prioritizes safer tactical plays.

Dice rolls are public, while programmed actions remain private until they resolve. Bot difficulty describes its strategy, not a guaranteed skill rating.

Setup choices

Mutators

Last Survivor is the default mode. The last standing player wins. A match can also end when every bomb has been cleared.

Last Survivor — 8 Players uses the same victory rules and locks the seat count to eight. Your other selected settings stay in place. Select Last Survivor again to change the seat count.

Playing sequence

Choose a designated order to assign seats manually, or random order to shuffle once at the start of the match. The order remains stable across rounds.

Defaults

Four seats, one human, Moderate bots, random order, 5 HP, Default visibility, 16 walls, and 20% hidden bombs.

Rounds and actions

  1. Every living player rolls a d6 publicly in playing order.
  2. Players secretly spend those points on a variable-length action program.
  3. The first action resolves for every player in playing order, then the next action does the same.
  4. Victory is checked after every action. Otherwise, the next round begins.

Movement and combat

  • Move: 1 point per grid, using all 8 directions.
  • Attack: reach up to 2 grids straight or 1 diagonally and push the nearest player.
  • Dodge: choose an adjacent sidestep. It can respond to an earlier player's attack in the same execution step; otherwise it activates on your turn for the next attack. Each Dodge is consumed once, even if blocked. Later-step Dodges cannot respond early.
  • Disarm: safely check and remove a bomb in one adjacent grid.

Minefield actions

  • Plant Bomb: place a bomb on an adjacent non-terrain grid.
  • Break Wall: destroy an adjacent wall for 2 points.
  • Scan Here: spend 1 point to record a public neighboring-bomb count and gain one adjustment to your next programmed Move this round. When that Move executes, click an adjacent highlighted grid or keep its original direction. Resolve Remaining pauses for this choice. No extra movement; adjustments do not stack.
  • 3-Grid Scan: reveal bombs directly in three grids, without numbers.
  • 3×3 Scan: reveal bombs and safe grids in a nine-grid patch, without numbers.

Board and visibility

The board is 10 x 10. Walls and ridges block movement; walls can be destroyed, while ridges are indestructible. Bombs remain hidden until discovered by movement, scanning, or Disarm.

  • Clear: explored grids stay known for the whole game.
  • Default: explored grids become unknown after two rounds.
  • Hard: explored grids become unknown after one round.

Scan clues are dated snapshots. A clue counts neighboring bombs at the time of the scan and does not prove that its own grid is safe.

Before the first roll, every starting tile receives a free public neighboring-bomb count. These opening scans grant no movement adjustment. Unused adjustments from Scan Here expire at round end.

Victory conditions

The last living player wins immediately. If more than one player remains and no bombs are left, the survivor with the highest HP wins; tied leaders share victory. Every bomb must be cleared, including bombs planted during the match.

From round 21 onward, triggering a mine causes instant death. Attack collision damage remains 1 HP.
